🔍 Truth and verification

The 10 commands in family 01 — from /truth a /freshness. Together, they form the complete antidote to hallucination and false certainty.

💎 /truth — separate fact from inference

The protocol for /truth: verifies assumptions, avoids stating unsupported claims as facts, and reports confidence. It’s the language’s most-used command because it solves the most common problem—fluent answers mixing truths with assumptions.

New here? One inference is a conclusion drawn indirectly from data ("if sales went up and the price didn’t change, the customer base grew"). A fact is something that has been directly verified. Inferences are valid — as long as labeled as inference.

📦 The default /truth output

  • •verified_facts — what the evidence supports.
  • •inferences — conclusions drawn but not verified.
  • •unknowns — what no one knows yet (and what matters).
  • •confidence — stated level of certainty.

🎯 Copy and run

/truth sobre

How to verify: four separate blocks (facts, inferences, unknowns, confidence) and no inference dressed up as fact.

2

✅ /verify — confirm the specific claim

While /truth audits a set, /verify target a claim: looks for enough evidence to confirm, correct, or reject. The result is always one of these three verbs—never "it seems so."

✓ Correct usage

  • ✓Claim specific and testable (“competitor X launched a plan in March”).
  • ✓Accept all three outcomes: confirmed, corrected, rejected.

✗ Incorrect usage

  • ✗Ask for verification of something vague ("check whether our marketing is working").
  • ✗Rejecting the answer for being inconclusive—inconclusive is data, not failure.

💡 Practical tip

Chain them when the stakes are high: /verify -> /confidence. A low-confidence confirmation calls for a second source before the decision.

3

🧱 /evidence — require evidentiary support

/evidence shows what evidence supports each important conclusion and where data is still missing. This is the command that turns a well-written opinion into an auditable argument.

✓ Evidence-backed argument

  • ✓Each conclusion lists its evidence (data, documents, measurements).
  • ✓Declared gaps: "no retention data beyond 60 days."

✗ Argument without evidence

  • ✗Safe conclusions with no evidence cited.
  • ✗"Studies show…" without saying which ones, from when, or involving whom.

🎯 Copy and run

/evidence para cada conclusão em:

How to verify: conclusion → evidence → gap map. Conclusions without evidence must be explicitly identified as weak points.

4

📚 /sources — prioritize sources by authority

The protocol organizes sources according to the official criteria: authority, recency, proximity to the facts, and independence.

📊 The four prioritization criteria

Authority

Who has recognized expertise on the subject?

Timeliness

Is the information valid at the time of the decision?

Proximity

Is it a primary source or seventh-hand?

Independence

Repeated citations count as a single source.

🎯 Copy and run

/sources para o tema ; ranqueie por autoridade, atualidade e independência

How to verify: ordered list with source-based justification and a warning about interdependent sources.

5

🔎 /factcheck — audit claims one by one

Breaks the content into verifiable claims and classifies each into five categories. New here? One claim is a claim that can be true or false — "the product costs R$ 99" is a claim; "the product is expensive" is an opinion.

The /factcheck pipeline: raw text goes in, verdicts for each claim come out—with opinions kept separate from facts.

🎯 Copy and run

/factcheck in the article:  — classify each claim and highlight the false ones

How to verify: table: claim → verdict → evidence used. Zero unclassified claims.

6

🎚️ /confidence — calibrate your confidence

Assigns a confidence level to conclusions and explains which factors would most increase or reduce that confidence. Calibrated confidence is different from a confident tone: it's a number accompanied by the conditions that would change it.

⚠️ Watch out for the classic mistake

Long, structured, and safe response isn’t right answer. Without explicit calibration, fluency becomes false credibility — exactly what this family exists to prevent.

🎯 Copy and run

/confidence nas conclusões de ; diga o que elevaria e o que reduziria cada grau

How to verify: each conclusion with a confidence level + review triggers ("with quarterly churn data, I'd raise it to high").

7

🌫️ /uncertainty + /assumptions — surface what you don't know

/uncertainty surfaces missing data, unknown variables, and points with more than one plausible interpretation. /assumptions lists the reasoning assumptions marked as verified, reasonable, or weak.

✓ Sound assumptions hygiene

  • ✓Each assumption has a declared status before the analysis runs.
  • ✓Weak assumption → low-cost test defined to validate it.

✗ Hidden assumption

  • ✗Entire analysis supported by "the market keeps growing" — without labeling or testing it.
  • ✗Uncertainty treated as something shameful to hide, not as information.

💡 Practical tip

Run /assumptions BEFORE expensive analyses (/deepresearch, /model). Finding a fragile assumption early saves the entire analysis.

8

🧬 /provenance + /freshness — origin and validity

The last two commands in the family complete the cycle: where it came from each critical piece of information (primary, secondary, inferred, or user-provided) and whether it still holds for today.

1

/factcheck — classify the claims

Breaks the text into claims and gives each a verdict. The foundation of everything.

2

/confidence — weigh the result

Confidence for each conclusion, with factors that would change it. Avoids performative certainty.

3

/provenance + /freshness — seal the origin and timeframe

Source of each critical data point + warning about what may have changed. Only then is the information ready for a decision.

🎯 Complete audit in one line

/chain(/factcheck -> /confidence -> /provenance -> /freshness) no relatório anexado

How to verify: final report with a verdict for each claim, confidence, source, and temporal validity — ready for a decision or publication.

💡 Practical tip

Using a 3-year-old statistic as current is the most common pitch deck slip-up. /freshness before the meeting, it takes 1 minute.

🎓 Module Summary

✓
/truth is the foundation — facts, inferences, and unknowns never share the same paragraph.
✓
Prove before you persuade — /verify, /evidence, and /sources turn opinions into auditable arguments.
✓
Calibrated confidence — number + conditions, never just tone of voice.
✓
Source and timeframe — /provenance and /freshness determine whether the information still deserves weight in the decision.
