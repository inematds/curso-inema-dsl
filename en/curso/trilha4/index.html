📐 Planning and architecture

The 10 commands in family 06 — from an executable objective to systematic verification.

What it is:

Turns an objective into an executable structure: phases, deliverables, and completion criteria for each stage.

Why learn:

An objective without a blueprint is a wish; with a blueprint, it’s a contract with verifiable steps.

Key concepts:

phase · deliverable · acceptance criteria.

What it is:

Puts phases in chronological order and sets verifiable milestones — dates with objective completion criteria.

Why learn:

A milestone without criteria is a birthday; a milestone with criteria is a decision point.

Key concepts:

verifiable milestone · sequence · time window.

What it is:

Reveals what needs to exist before what—including external dependencies outside your control.

Why learn:

Ignoring the critical path turns a small delay into a total delay.

Key concepts:

critical path · prerequisite · external blocker.

What it is:

Estimate the actual inputs: people, data, tools, and money for each phase.

Why learn:

A plan without execution costs is a marketing plan.

Key concepts:

estimate by phase · buffer · resource constraint.

What it is:

Define components, responsibilities, and contracts between them—before coding or hiring.

Why learn:

A poorly defined interface compounds costs throughout the project.

Key concepts:

component · responsibility · contract between parts.

What it is:

Model the process as states and transitions—each item knows where it is and where it’s going.

Why learn:

A process without states leads to items being "in progress" for three months.

Key concepts:

state · transition · transition condition.

What it is:

Turns a recurring process into a written standard procedure—executable by someone who has never done it before.

Why learn:

An SOP is what separates a business that scales from one that depends on its owner.

Key concepts:

atomic step · owner for each step · exception handled.

What it is:

Generate checklists for known failure points and optimize task order.

Why learn:

Aviation has used checklists for decades because human memory fails precisely in routine situations.

Key concepts:

failure point · binary item · optimal order.

4.2~75 min

🚀 Execution and operations

The 10 commands in family 07 — from turning the plan into actions to reviewing against criteria.

What it is:

Turns the plan into a sequence of concrete actions, with an order and expected result for each step.

Why learn:

It’s the bridge between the planning track and the physical world where things happen.

Key concepts:

actionable step · explicit order · verifiable output.

What it is:

Among everything that could be done, point to ONE next action—the highest-value one right now.

Why learn:

Decision paralysis from too many options is the most common bottleneck for people working alone.

Key concepts:

one-time action · rationale for choice · current state.

What it is:

Breaks deliverables into finishable tasks and assigns a single owner to each one.

Why learn:

A task with two owners has no owner; a task with no defined end never gets finished.

Key concepts:

finishable task · single owner · definition of done.

What it is:

Produces estimates with ranges and assumptions — "2–3 days, if X happens" instead of "Tuesday".

Why learn:

An overly precise number hides real uncertainty and undermines trust when it’s wrong.

Key concepts:

range · stated assumption · margin.

What it is:

Sorts the queue by expected impact and risk—not by who shouts the loudest.

Why learn:

Implicit priority always goes to the latest urgency received.

Key concepts:

impact × risk · ordered queue · deliberate cutoff.

What it is:

Identify what can run simultaneously and what should become a stable automation.

Why learn:

Automating an unstable process automates chaos—stabilize first, then automate.

Key concepts:

parallelizable vs. serial · automation candidate · maintenance cost.

What it is:

Define what to observe, how often, and what threshold triggers an alert or action.

Why learn:

A metric without a threshold is dashboard decoration; the threshold defines when to respond.

Key concepts:

indicator · threshold · check cadence.

What it is:

Compare the deliverable with the original criteria and make corrections—not just offer opinions.

Why learn:

Review against criteria closes the loop opened earlier by /blueprint.

Key concepts:

acceptance criteria · gap · traceable correction.
