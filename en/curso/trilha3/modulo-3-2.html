🎯 Comparison and decision

The commands in family 05: criteria before options, justified rankings, trade-off matrices, and bets with an exit condition.

📏 /criteria + /weights — define how to choose

The golden rule of family 05: the criteria and their weights exist before for the options to be evaluated. A yardstick set afterward is designed to favor the preferred measure.

📐 Anatomy of a good criterion

  • •Measurable: “time to first paying customer,” not “agility.”
  • •Weighted: impact 40%, cost 30%, risk 30% — stated weights, not implied.
  • •Independent: criteria that duplicate the same factor inflate the score without adding information.

New here? Weight is the relative value of each criterion in the final score — the numerical translation of "this matters more to us." Undeclared weights don’t disappear: they become invisible bias.

🎯 Copy and run

/criteria /weights para escolher ; proponha critérios mensuráveis e pesos com justificativa

How to verify: you agree with the weights after reading them on your own — if they surprise you, discuss them now, not later.

2

🥇 /rank — rank alternatives with justification

Sorts options by criteria and explains each position. A ranking without justification is a numbered preference; with parameters, it becomes an auditable team standard.

🎯 Copy and run

/rank criteria=impacto,custo,risco entre ,  e ; justifique cada posição

How to verify: swap two items and see whether the rationale still holds together — a fragile ranking falls apart under the test.

💡 Practical tip

Ask for sensitivity: "if the risk weight doubles, who wins?" A ranking that changes completely with small weight variations is a technical tie in disguise.

3

🔢 /score — score on an explicit scale

Scores by criterion in scale with anchors (“7 = we already have a pilot customer in this range”). Without an anchor, each evaluator uses a different yardstick, and the average becomes noise.

1

Set anchors for each level

Describe what scores of 3, 6, and 9 mean for each criterion—concrete examples, not adjectives.

2

Show the composition

Always show the total score alongside the partial scores — a win based on one dominant criterion should be visible.

🎯 Copy and run

/score as opções , ,  em escala 1-10 por criterio com âncoras; mostre composição da nota final

How to verify: you can reconstruct the decision by looking only at the table — without reading the prose.

4

⚖️ /compare — compare without deciding too soon

Produces a side-by-side comparison by dimension, without forcing a verdict. Separating comparison from decision preserves the window in which alternatives can still surprise you.

✓ Open comparison

  • ✓Same dimensions for all options—including those that favor the underdog.
  • ✓"Differences that matter" highlighted separately from cosmetic differences.

✗ Premature conclusion

  • ✗A comparison written to confirm the option the boss has already chosen.
  • ✗Different dimensions for each option—a table that hides what’s uncomfortable.

🎯 Copy and run

/compare  vs  nas dimensões: custo, curva de aprendizado, lock-in e suporte. Só compare, não decida

How to verify: symmetric table, same set of dimensions, no recommendation embedded in the text.

5

🏁 /decide — make decisions with evidence and reversibility

Close the process with a single recommendation, evidence, and—crucially—the degree of reversibility: how much would it cost to reverse course if you’re wrong?

Illustrative: the first question for any decision is "which door am I going through?"

💡 Practical tip

The family’s complete chain: /chain(/criteria -> /rank -> /decide). The decide inherits the criteria set at the start—criteria that can’t be adjusted retroactively to favor the preferred option.

🎯 Copy and run

/decide entre ,  e  usando os critérios definidos acima; declare reversibilidade e custo de mudar de ideia

How to verify: one recommendation (no “it depends”), with cited evidence and a stated plan B.

6

🔲 /tradeoffmatrix — compare alternatives × criteria

The complete matrix: each alternative assessed against each criterion, with weights applied and every cell justified. Well-crafted narratives fall apart in the face of a well-built matrix.

Criterion (weight)Option AOption BOption C
Impact (40%)8 · broad reach6 · medium term9 · new market
Cost (30%)5 · new team8 · reuses the team3 · high capex
Risk (30%)7 · proven technology8 · single dependency4 · regulatory uncertainty

Teaching example: each cell = rating + reason on one line. The matrix shows that C wins on impact but bleeds on cost and risk.

🎯 Copy and run

/tradeoffmatrix entre ; linhas=critérios com pesos, colunas=opções, cada célula com nota e motivo

How to verify: no empty cells and no generic "great" — every rating has a verifiable reason.

7

📈 /pareto — find the dominant frontier

Eliminates the dominated options — those that another option matches or beats on every criterion—and the frontier where improving one criterion requires sacrificing another.

New here? In Pareto frontier there’s no free lunch: to gain on one axis, you pay on another. A dominated option is one that loses to another option on EVERY axis — it should never have made it to the decision table.

🧹 What the command eliminates

  • •Your most expensive and slowest internal competitor compared with another proposal of yours.
  • •The tool that covers the other one in both price AND functionality.
  • •The project someone else delivers just as well, sooner, and with fewer people.

🎯 Copy and run

/pareto sobre as opções listadas; elimine dominadas, mostre a fronteira e diga o trade-off de cada sobrevivente

How to verify: shortened list with justification based on the eliminated option's dominance — not preference.

8

🎲 /bet + /stop — place a bet and know when to stop

Every big bet raises three questions: how much could it yield (upside), how much it might cost (downside) e when we stop? /bet structures the first two; /stop locks the third before emotion kicks in.

✓ A grown-up bet

  • ✓"If we don’t have 10 paying customers in 90 days, we’ll shut down the line."
  • ✓Objective trigger, decided when no one is emotionally invested.

✗ Zombie project

  • ✗"Let’s give it one more quarter" — the fourth in a row, with no new criteria.
  • ✗A stopping criterion that shifts with the results (“when it’s good enough”).

🎯 Copy and run

/bet /stop para apostar em ; upside, downside, exposição máxima e kill criteria objetivos com data

How to verify: kill criteria that a stranger could judge without emotional context — number + date.

⚠️ The stranger test

Show your stop criterion to someone uninvolved: looking at the numbers objectively, could they tell whether the time has come? If they need "context," the criterion is just for show.

Quick self-check (optional): why define criteria BEFORE seeing the options?

🎓 Module Summary

✓
Set the bar before the race — criteria and weights set before the options enter the arena.
✓
Ranking with memory — /rank, /score, and the matrix justify each position and cell.
✓
Pareto shortens the queue — dominated options are removed before they waste meeting time.
✓
Bet with an exit — upside, downside, and kill criteria with a number and a date.
